Research

Design and optimisation of dual CAR-NK cells for breast cancer using AI/ML

M.Tech thesis, Stem Cell and Cancer Biology Group, IIT Guwahati, 2025–26. Supervised by Prof. Bithiah Grace Jaganathan and Dr. Suresh Sundaram. A two-phase computational framework that identifies, structurally validates and ranks dual-antigen targets for tandem CAR-NK cells across the four PAM50 breast cancer subtypes.

Phase I — target discovery

  1. TranscriptomicsTCGA-BRCA STAR counts from the GDC: 1,224 samples (1,111 primary tumour, 113 solid-tissue normal), filtered to 16,834 protein-coding genes and assigned PAM50 subtypes with genefu.
  2. Differential expressionSubtype-versus-normal DESeq2 (padj < 0.05, log2FC ≥ 1) giving 2,186–3,180 upregulated genes per subtype, cross-checked with limma-voom and GEO2R.
  3. Surfaceome and safetyIntersection with a 2,304-gene Human Protein Atlas surfaceome, a four-tier GTEx v8 normal-tissue safety classification yielding 325 Tier A antigens, then CARTAR logic-gate pairing and literature and patent tractability screening — nine final tandem-CAR pairs.

Phase II — structural validation

  1. scFv modellingSeven antigens modelled in both VH–(G4S)3–VL and VL–(G4S)3–VH orientations with AlphaFold2 (ColabFold, mean pLDDT > 90), CDRs annotated via SAbPred/IMGT and membrane topology via DeepTMHMM.
  2. Docking and rankingCDR-restrained HADDOCK 2.4 docking with ClusPro as a blind check, plus AlphaFold3 three-chain co-docking for six pairs. A six-parameter weighted composite score ranked CEACAM5–CLDN9 and PSCA–CEACAM5 as the leading Grade A candidates.
  3. Construct designDe novo KISS1R binder scaffolds generated with RFdiffusion and ProteinMPNN, and four complete NK-optimised CAR sequences assembled (CD8α signal peptide and hinge/transmembrane domain, 2B4 co-stimulatory domain, CD3ζ).

Everything above is computational prioritisation. None of the candidate pairs or constructs has been validated experimentally, and the ranking is a hypothesis-generating result rather than a claim about clinical utility.

The final thesis pipeline and its data are deliberately not published, because the work is being prepared for possible publication. An earlier build of the transcriptomics stage is public on GitHub as TCGA-BRCA-pipeline, with its false positives documented.

2023 – 2025

Genetic underpinnings of adverse events across populations

Department of Pharmacology, DIPSAR/DPSRU · with Asees Singh, guided by Dr. Anoop Kumar

  • Co-conceptualised a population-level test of whether the adverse-reaction profiles of four ACE inhibitors track genetic variation across the US, UK and Canada.
  • Curated the analysis datasets: over 3,000 OpenVigil (FDA FAERS) adverse-event records stratified by region, sex and age, alongside SmPC-listed reactions, MedDRA organ-system classes and 1000 Genomes variant data.
  • The study found a single significant correlation after adjustment, which points to no population-level genetic association. Manuscript drafted and awaiting review by the corresponding author.

B.Pharm project: RP-UFLC method for caffeine quantification

Department of Pharmaceutics, DIPSAR/DPSRU · Guide: Dr. Beauty Behera

  • Developed a reversed-phase UFLC method for caffeine in a commercial soft drink on a BDS C18 column with an acetonitrile–phosphate buffer mobile phase.
  • Evaluated the ICH Q2(R1) validation parameters: linearity from 20 to 100 ppm, limits of detection and quantification, intra- and inter-day precision and spike recovery.
